      Horror Engine

      A playable machine for horror sound, by Garyck Arntzen (x.com/_mintydoge). Every sound is synthesised live from physical models. Inspired by Mark Korven's Apprehension Engine.

      Playing

      • Pick a voice, then drag on the surface. Bowed and scraped voices are silent until you move.
      • Hold keeps a voice sounding hands-free. Aura replays the machine's own recent sound as a pad; Freeze pins it.
      • Mouse pressure: hold Shift for hard, Alt for soft, or roll the wheel over the surface. A pen uses its real pressure.
      • Knobs: drag up and down, Shift for fine, double-click to reset, arrow keys when focused.

      The machine

      • Machine V2. Drag any part to play it. Stroke the rulers, rods, coil or saw to bow them; tap them to strike (drag up or down while bowing the saw to bend it). Grab the brass tray by its rim and push one end down to rock it: the water runs to the low side and bends those rods down, and it sways back level when you let go (Shift keeps the tilt). While bowing the rods, push up or down to rock the tray under the note. Draw on the slate with chalk, rub the bottle with a wet finger (Alt for a nail on either). Lift a superball off its wand and drag it onto the thunder sheet, the rods, the drumhead or the box. Hold the glass tap to fill the wine glass (Shift drains it). Pull the drawer open to rake the junk. Home and F6–F9 move the camera, K follows the part you play, D is demo mode. The first Machine is at ?machine=v1.
      • Shared body. Like the real Apprehension Engine, every part is bolted to one wooden box heard through contact mics. Body sets how much the box rings along; Piezo moves from the warm box to the quacky, bass-light contact-mic sound.
      • Harmonizer. Each voice has a Harmonizer send (in its Channel knobs). The return is pitch-shifted (an octave down by default), split into two detuned voices, with feedback for falling spirals.
      • Superball (IX). A rubber ball on a stick dragged over a drumhead, a steel sheet or the cabinet. It moans; pressure raises the pitch, and it catches on the surface's own notes.
      • Blaster Beam. Set the E-bow's Excite to Strike or Pluck (or play the long beam on the floor in the Machine view): a hit rings for seconds and x slides the bar.
      • Water. Rods, bells and the steel sheet wobble in pitch as if standing in a waterphone's bowl. Slosh speeds the wobble.
      • Rulers are pitched by their overhang (Ruler length): 10 cm is about 65 Hz, 20 cm about 16 Hz. Bowed metal jumps between modes when you change bow pressure.
      • Spring tank echoes drip downward, highs first and lows last, like a real tank. Its E-bow mode holds it in sustain.

      Recording

      Record captures the limited master mix at the audio device rate. With Stems on you also get one stereo file per voice, the hall return, the reverse swell, the harmonizer return and the body. Stems are taken after Distance and the master tone, volume and low cut, but before the limiter; they line up with the mix sample for sample and add up to it. 32-bit float is the default; 24-bit stems are padded by 6 dB so they cannot clip. Stop to save a zip of WAV files with the session settings alongside. Mix-only takes run up to 10 minutes, takes with stems up to 4.
