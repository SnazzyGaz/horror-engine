      Horror Engine

      A playable machine for horror sound, by Garyck Arntzen (x.com/_mintydoge). Every sound is synthesised live from physical models. Inspired by Mark Korven's Apprehension Engine.

      Playing

      • Pick a voice, then drag on the surface. Bowed and scraped voices are silent until you move.
      • Hold keeps a voice sounding hands-free. Aura replays the machine's own recent sound as a pad; Freeze pins it.
      • Mouse pressure: hold Shift for hard, Alt for soft, or roll the wheel over the surface. A pen uses its real pressure.
      • Knobs: drag up and down, Shift for fine, double-click to reset, arrow keys when focused.
      • Undo (Ctrl+Z, or ↶ ↷ in the top bar) steps back through knob and switch moves, presets, loaded files, share links, Defaults, Reset and looper changes, one timeline, 200 steps. A knob drag is one step.
      • Link copies a short address that rebuilds every setting (your sound-in settings stay yours). Open it, or paste it into the address bar, to load them; Undo puts yours back.
      • Each voice has Tune & place in its panel, under Channel: Tune in semitones, Fine in cents, and Material and Distance offsets. These add on top of the Macros, so you can retune or recede one instrument without moving the whole machine. A brass dot on the voice marks an offset. Reset on that panel clears that voice; Defaults under the voice list clears every voice (click Sure? to confirm; Undo then appears for a few seconds).

      Your own moments

      • Press Record moment, play anything on any voice (Surface or Machine) and move any knobs or macros, then press it again. Silence before your first touch is trimmed. Name it and Save: it becomes a button after the built-in five, on F6 onwards.
      • Press it to play it once through the models. Like a loop layer it is performed again each time, with a little ghost-style variation in timing, position, pressure and depth, so no two firings match. Press it again while it plays to stop it.
      • Knob and macro moves ride on top of where your knobs are now (relative), so a Dread swell recorded from 20% to 60% lifts today’s Dread by the same amount. Switches and choices (Hold, Excite, Object…) are set exactly. Your knobs never jump: move one during a moment and it becomes the new base, and every knob is back where you left it when the moment ends.
      • The … menu on each moment: rename, play it on another voice, export as a .horror-moment.json file, or delete. Moments are kept in this browser and saved inside presets and performance files; Render replays them.

      The machine

      • Surface first. The page opens on the Surface so you can play at once, while the 3D Machine warms up behind it (the note beside the tabs). When it is ready the tab lights up; if you have not started playing the Surface, the view switches over by itself. Add ?view=machine to the address to open on the Machine and its loading screen instead.
      • The Machine. Drag any part to play it. Stroke the rulers, rods, coil or saw to bow them; tap them to strike (drag up or down while bowing the saw to bend it). Grab the brass tray by its rim and push one end down to rock it: the water runs to the low side and bends those rods down, and it sways back level when you let go (Shift keeps the tilt). While bowing the rods, push up or down to rock the tray under the note. Draw on the slate with chalk, rub the bottle with a wet finger (Alt for a nail on either). Lift a superball off its wand and drag it onto the thunder sheet, the rods, the drumhead or the box. Hold the glass tap to fill the wine glass (Shift drains it). Pull the drawer open to rake the junk. Home and Shift+F6–Shift+F9 move the camera, K follows the part you play, D is demo mode.
      • Shared body. Like the real Apprehension Engine, every part is bolted to one wooden box heard through contact mics. Body sets how much the box rings along; Piezo moves from the warm box to the quacky, bass-light contact-mic sound.
      • Harmonizer. Each voice has a Harmonizer send (in its Channel knobs). The return is pitch-shifted (an octave down by default), split into two detuned voices, with feedback for falling spirals.
      • Superball (IX). A rubber ball on a stick dragged over a drumhead, a steel sheet or the cabinet. It moans; pressure raises the pitch, and it catches on the surface's own notes.
      • Blaster Beam. Set the E-bow's Excite to Strike or Pluck (or play the long beam on the floor in the Machine view): a hit rings for seconds and x slides the bar.
      • Water. Rods, bells and the steel sheet wobble in pitch as if standing in a waterphone's bowl. Slosh speeds the wobble.
      • Rulers are pitched by their overhang (Ruler length): 10 cm is about 65 Hz, 20 cm about 16 Hz. Bowed metal jumps between modes when you change bow pressure.
      • Spring tank echoes drip downward, highs first and lows last, like a real tank. Its E-bow mode holds it in sustain.

      Recording

      Record captures the limited master mix at the audio device rate. With Stems on you also get one stereo file per voice, the hall return, the reverse swell, the harmonizer return and the body. Stems are taken after Distance and the master tone, volume and low cut, but before the limiter; they line up with the mix sample for sample and add up to it. 32-bit float is the default; 24-bit stems are padded by 6 dB so they cannot clip. Stop to save a zip of WAV files with the session settings alongside. Mix-only takes run up to 10 minutes, takes with stems up to 4.

      Save what I just played (Shift+R or Last min): you do not have to press Record first. The last minute of the mix is always kept, so this saves it as a WAV with its performance file. Render that file with Stems on to get the same minute as stems. In the first five minutes of a session the stems add up to the saved mix exactly; after that the file starts from a checkpoint a little before the minute (the looper, knobs, held notes and running moments are rebuilt there), so the stems are a fresh performance of the same moves (the noisy and random parts of the models differ, so they will not add up to the saved WAV), and rendering stays quick however long you play.

      Your sound in

      Press Your sound in on the Hall & Master strip, then Mic / line (the browser asks for permission; echo cancelling, noise suppression and auto gain are off) or File. Your sound goes through the spring tank, the body, the hall, the harmonizer and Aura; Dry is its own level in the mix. Stop or Silence (Esc) ends it. Recordings with stems get an extra input stem. Render cannot replay your sound, so a performance file made while it was on says so, and re-renders the machine alone.

      MIDI

      Where the browser allows it: notes play the selected voice, CC 20–23 move Dread, Distance, Material and Pitch. MIDI learn: right-click any knob, switch or selector (long-press on a touch screen), choose MIDI learn, then move a control on your controller. CC 0–119 on any channel can be learned; a learned CC wins over the fixed ones on its channel, one CC drives one control, and the menu forgets one or all. Learned controls show a small dot. Mappings are kept in this browser. Moments: notes 36–127 on MIDI channel 16 fire moment 1, 2, 3… (36–40 the built-in five, 41 your first own one); or CC 24–43 on any channel fire the first twenty moments when the value crosses 64 upwards. Other MIDI channels still play notes normally. Firing a playing moment again stops it.
